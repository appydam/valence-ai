An **AI orchestrator** is the coordinator agent in a multi-agent system. When a human submits a goal, the orchestrator parses it into subtasks, routes each subtask to the appropriate specialist agent, monitors execution progress, handles failures and edge cases, and assembles final deliverables for delivery. The orchestrator holds the "mission mental model" — the full picture of what needs to happen and why — while specialists focus on their narrow areas of expertise.

In Valence AI's architecture, **Kaze** is the orchestrator agent — the AI that coordinates Scout (research), Forge (building), Ghost (writing), and Sentinel (quality review) on every mission.

## What an AI Orchestrator Does

**Goal parsing:** The orchestrator receives a natural language mission and interprets what it requires — what information needs to be gathered, what outputs need to be produced, what systems need to be updated, what quality standards apply.

**Task decomposition:** The orchestrator breaks the goal into discrete subtasks with clear inputs, expected outputs, and dependencies. Parallelizable tasks are identified for simultaneous execution; sequential tasks are ordered by dependency.

**Agent routing:** Each subtask is assigned to the appropriate specialist. Research tasks go to Scout. Writing tasks go to Ghost. System operations go to Forge. Quality review goes to Sentinel.

**Progress monitoring:** The orchestrator tracks which tasks are complete, in progress, or blocked. When an agent encounters an error, the orchestrator determines whether to retry, try an alternate approach, or escalate.

**Failure handling:** When tasks fail (API error, quality rejection, agent timeout), the orchestrator manages recovery: retrying transient failures, routing around permanent failures, escalating when the retry budget is exhausted.

**Output assembly:** When all subtasks are complete and outputs have passed quality review, the orchestrator assembles the final deliverable and coordinates delivery to the human stakeholder.

## Why Orchestration Is Separate from Specialization

The orchestrator could theoretically do the research, writing, and building itself — but performance is better when orchestration is separated from specialization. The orchestrator's cognitive task (planning, routing, monitoring, adapting) is fundamentally different from a specialist's cognitive task (deep research synthesis, quality writing, precise system operations). Keeping them separate allows each to be optimized for its function.

Additionally, the orchestrator needs a broader, higher-level view of the mission than specialists need. A specialist agent only needs context for its specific subtask; the orchestrator needs the full mission context. Separating roles prevents specialist agents from being loaded with irrelevant context from other parts of the mission.

## Orchestrator Quality

The quality of the orchestrator is the primary determinant of multi-agent system quality. A poor orchestrator that creates ambiguous tasks, misses parallelization opportunities, or fails to handle errors will produce poor outcomes even with excellent specialist agents. The orchestrator's planning and routing quality is the multiplier on the entire system's capability.
