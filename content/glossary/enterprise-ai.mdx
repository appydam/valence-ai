**Enterprise AI** refers to AI systems and platforms designed for organizational deployment at scale — characterized by the security, compliance, integration depth, reliability, and governance features that enterprise IT and legal requirements demand. Enterprise AI is the category that separates AI tools suitable for individual use from AI platforms suitable for handling sensitive business data and consequential business workflows at scale.

The "enterprise" qualifier isn't about capability alone — consumer AI models (Claude, GPT-4) are highly capable. It's about the deployment infrastructure, compliance posture, and organizational features required when AI is embedded in business operations.

## What Makes AI "Enterprise"

### Infrastructure

**Dedicated infrastructure:** Consumer AI tools run on shared infrastructure — your data processes alongside other customers' data. Enterprise AI platforms provide dedicated compute, isolated storage, and private networking per customer. Data never commingles across organizational boundaries.

**Uptime and SLA:** Enterprise deployments require guaranteed availability (99.9%+ SLA) with defined response times for incidents. Consumer tools have no SLA.

**Data residency:** Some organizations and regulatory environments require data to remain within specific geographic boundaries. Enterprise AI platforms offer regional deployment options.

### Compliance

**SOC 2 Type II:** The standard audit for enterprise security controls — validates that the vendor has adequate controls for security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy. Required by most enterprise IT procurement processes.

**GDPR/CCPA compliance:** Data processing agreements (DPAs) that define how personal data is processed and retained. Required for any AI system handling EU personal data or California resident data.

**Industry-specific:** HIPAA for healthcare, FedRAMP for government, PCI DSS for payment processing. Enterprise AI platforms serving regulated industries must achieve the relevant certifications.

### Access Control and Governance

**SSO/SAML integration:** Enterprise users authenticate through their existing identity provider (Okta, Azure AD), not through separate credentials.

**Role-based access control (RBAC):** Different users have different permissions — some can submit missions, others can only view results, admins can configure integrations.

**Audit logs:** Every agent action is logged with timestamp, user, action type, data accessed, and outcome. Logs are immutable and retained for the required period.

### Integration Depth

Enterprise workflows span many systems. Enterprise AI platforms provide deep, maintained integrations with the core enterprise stack: Salesforce, HubSpot, Workday, SAP, ServiceNow, Jira, Confluence, Microsoft 365, Google Workspace — not just consumer-grade API access.

### Support

Consumer AI tools offer community support and documentation. Enterprise AI platforms provide dedicated account management, implementation support, and SLA-backed incident response. When something breaks in a production workflow, there's a human to call.

## Enterprise AI in Practice

Enterprise AI deployments are characterized by:
- Formal security review and vendor assessment before deployment
- IT involvement in integration setup and access control configuration
- Change management and team training programs
- Governance policies defining who can use the system and for what purposes
- Regular security reviews and access audits
