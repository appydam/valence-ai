**Generative AI agents** are autonomous AI systems powered by large language models (LLMs) that combine content generation capabilities with agency — the ability to take actions in the world. A generative AI agent does not just produce text in response to a prompt; it uses tool calls, maintains memory, plans multi-step action sequences, and executes those plans to achieve goals.

The term "generative AI agent" bridges two concepts: **generative AI** (models that produce original content) and **AI agents** (systems that act autonomously). A generative AI agent does both — it generates content (emails, reports, code, analysis) as part of executing autonomous multi-step workflows.

## How Generative AI Agents Differ from Generative AI Models

A generative AI model (like a standalone LLM) produces content when prompted and stops. It has no memory between calls, no tools, and no ability to act on its outputs.

A generative AI agent wraps a generative model with:

**Tool use:** The agent can call APIs, read databases, search the web, execute code, send emails, update CRM records, and interact with any connected system.

**Planning:** The agent decomposes goals into subtasks, identifies dependencies, and determines the sequence of actions needed.

**Memory:** The agent maintains context within a session (working memory) and across sessions (episodic memory), building understanding of the user's business over time.

**Execution loop:** The agent acts, observes results, evaluates progress, and decides next steps — iterating until the goal is achieved.

**Reflection:** The agent evaluates the quality of its own outputs and adjusts its approach when results fall short.

## Types of Generative AI Agents in Business

**Research agents:** Generate intelligence briefs, competitive analyses, market summaries, and prospect profiles by synthesizing information from multiple sources.

**Writing agents:** Generate emails, blog posts, reports, social media content, case studies, and other written communications in a brand-consistent voice.

**Building agents:** Generate code, create pull requests, configure integrations, and automate technical workflows.

**Review agents:** Generate quality assessments, audit reports, and compliance evaluations by analyzing other agents' outputs against defined criteria.

**Orchestrator agents:** Generate task plans, decompose missions, route work to specialists, and synthesize final deliverables from multiple agent outputs.
