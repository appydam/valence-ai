Make (formerly Integromat) and Valence AI both connect software tools and automate business workflows — but they operate on fundamentally different principles. Make is a visual, no-code workflow automation platform where you build explicit, step-by-step sequences of actions triggered by events. Valence AI is an autonomous AI workforce where intelligent agents receive goals in natural language and figure out the steps themselves. The difference isn't just technical — it's the difference between programming a workflow and hiring a worker who can handle the workflow.

## What Is Make?

Make is a visual no-code/low-code workflow automation platform that connects 1,500+ apps through a drag-and-drop scenario builder. You define triggers (when X happens), actions (do Y), and filters/conditions (if Z then branch). Make executes your scenario exactly as designed.

Make is the evolution of IFTTT-style automation — more powerful, more flexible, capable of complex multi-step workflows with branches, iterators, and data transformations. It's excellent for connecting your software stack and moving data between applications on schedule or trigger.

**Make is excellent for:**
- Automating repetitive, rule-based data flows between apps
- Teams with technical operations staff who can build and maintain scenarios
- High-volume, low-complexity automation (thousands of records processed identically)
- Connecting 1,500+ apps that Valence AI doesn't cover
- Workflows with completely predictable inputs and outputs

## What Is Valence AI?

Valence AI is a production-ready enterprise platform that deploys five specialized AI agents — Kaze (orchestrator), Scout (researcher), Forge (builder), Ghost (writer), and Sentinel (monitor) — to handle complex business workflows that require reasoning, judgment, and adaptation. Business teams submit missions in natural language; the platform handles everything else.

## The Core Philosophical Difference

Make automates workflows you've already designed. Valence AI handles workflows you haven't fully specified.

With Make, you answer: "What should happen when X occurs?" You map every input, every output, every branch condition in advance. When reality deviates from your map — an API field changes, an unexpected value arrives, an edge case appears — Make errors.

With Valence AI, you answer: "What do I want to accomplish?" The agents figure out the path. If data is missing, Scout finds it. If the output isn't right, Sentinel flags it and Ghost revises. The workflow adapts in real time.

### What Make Can't Do That Valence AI Can

**Research and gather new information:** Make can pull data from connected apps, but it can't research information that doesn't already exist in your systems. Valence AI's Scout agent searches the web, analyzes documents, synthesizes findings, and adds new information to workflows.

**Write and create content:** Make routes data; it doesn't create it. Valence AI's Ghost agent writes emails, documents, reports, and summaries as part of any workflow.

**Make judgment calls:** Make applies rules. Valence AI's agents apply reasoning. "Is this lead worth prioritizing?" is a question Make can't answer (without you pre-defining complex scoring logic). Kaze can answer it with context from Scout's research.

**Handle exceptions gracefully:** Make fails on unexpected input. Valence AI agents reason through edge cases and find paths forward — then escalate to humans when genuinely stuck.

## What Make Does Better Than Valence AI

**Breadth of integrations:** 1,500+ apps vs. 100+. If you need to connect an obscure SaaS tool that Valence AI doesn't support, Make almost certainly has it.

**Per-operation cost:** Make's pricing is per-operation, making it far cheaper for high-volume, simple data flows (syncing thousands of records, processing form submissions at scale).

**Determinism:** When you need guaranteed identical behavior on every run — no variation, no adaptation — Make's explicit scenario logic is a feature, not a limitation.

**Existing workflows:** If you have working automation that processes predictable, rule-based tasks reliably, there's no reason to replace it.

## Make + Valence AI: The Hybrid Architecture

Many sophisticated operations teams use both. A common pattern:

1. **Make handles the pipes** — moving data between systems, triggering webhooks, processing high-volume identical records
2. **Valence AI handles the judgment** — research, writing, complex decision-making, exception handling
3. **Make triggers Valence AI** via webhook when an AI-powered workflow is needed (e.g., new high-value lead arrives → Make webhook → Valence AI researches and drafts personalized outreach)

This hybrid gives you Make's breadth and cost efficiency plus Valence AI's intelligence and adaptability.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Make no-code?

Make is low-code — most workflows can be built without writing code, but complex data transformations often require knowledge of Make's formula language and module configuration. Valence AI is truly no-code from the business user's perspective: submit a mission in plain English, receive deliverables.

### Does Valence AI replace Zapier or Make entirely?

For rule-based, high-volume data flows, no — Make and Zapier remain cost-effective for those use cases. Valence AI complements them by handling the workflows that require intelligence. The question to ask: "Does this workflow require judgment, research, or content creation?" If yes, Valence AI. If it's purely data routing, Make is fine.

### How does pricing compare at scale?

Make's pricing is per-operation (1,000 to 10M+ operations/month). At high volume, Make is significantly cheaper per unit. Valence AI's pricing is per-team/month, not per-operation. For complex workflows where you'd otherwise need human labor (research, writing, analysis), Valence AI delivers clear ROI. For data routing, Make wins on cost.

### Can Make handle AI tasks with AI modules?

Make offers OpenAI and other AI integrations — you can add an "ask ChatGPT" step to a scenario. But this is prompt-and-response, not multi-agent orchestration. There's no specialist agent structure, no persistent memory, no quality review loop, no autonomous task decomposition. An AI module in Make is a feature; Valence AI is an AI-native architecture.
